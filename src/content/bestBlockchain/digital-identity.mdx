## What "digital identity" means here

A system whose primary product is *being someone* — proof of attributes, verifiable credentials, account-as-identity flows, sign-in services, reputation systems, or sovereignty-oriented identity. Not products where identity is incidental to the main flow.

For these products, the chain's account model and profile layer are doing more architectural work than for any other category — and the matrix above weights those rows accordingly.

## The primitives identity systems ship

Identity systems on blockchain aren't a monolith; they're a set of specialist primitives, each shipped by a different named implementation. The comparison in this page groups them into six axes:

- **Naming**: human-readable handles for identities. ENS on Ethereum + L2s, Basenames on Base, SNS on Solana, LSP3 name key on LUKSO.
- **Profile metadata**: persistent data about the user (image, links, custom fields). ENS text records, Farcaster user data, Lens ProfileNFT metadata, LSP3 typed keys on LUKSO.
- **Social graph**: standardized follower or interaction protocol at the chain level. Farcaster FIDs on Optimism, Lens on Polygon, LSP26 on LUKSO — none at chain level on Ethereum L1 or the other L2s.
- **Attestations**: verifiable claims issued by one party about another. EAS canonical on Optimism (portable across EVM), Polygon ID for ZK-native claims, Civic for KYC, World ID for personhood, ERC-725Y claim keys on LUKSO.
- **Account model**: whether identity is an EOA, a retrofitted smart account, or a smart account by design. EOA + ERC-4337 on Ethereum and L2s, EIP-7702 for delegated EOAs, Coinbase Smart Wallet on Base, keypair + per-program on Solana, LSP0 smart account by default on LUKSO.
- **Personhood / Sybil resistance**: cryptographic guarantee of one identity per human. Worldcoin World ID via iris scan, BrightID via social graph, Gitcoin Passport as an aggregate stamp system, Civic via KYC — composable across chains as attestations.

Different products bind on different primitives. A wallet product binds on account model and recovery. A creator platform binds on naming and profile metadata. A social app binds on the social graph. An airdrop or quadratic-voting mechanism binds on Sybil resistance. Reading the matrix means asking which primitive your product binds on and picking the chain that ships it best.

## Where each identity system wins, and where it doesn't

**ENS (Ethereum Name Service).** The network-effect leader for naming on Ethereum and every EVM L2. Wallets recognize `.eth` names universally, text records let holders publish profile data at the DNS layer of Web3, and the resolver architecture is battle-tested. Cons relative to a chain-level profile: ENS is a naming and pointer system, not an account model or a permission system. Profile data assembles per-app on top; the user's smart-account architecture, session-key model, and recovery flow all live somewhere else. ENS pairs well with a chain-level profile substrate but doesn't replace one.

**Basenames + Coinbase Smart Wallet (Base).** Coinbase's identity stack ships naming (Basenames), a passkey-based smart wallet, and mainstream consumer distribution. For a product whose users are already in the Coinbase ecosystem, the onboarding gradient is the shallowest of any EVM chain. Cons: identity primitives beyond naming and account model still assemble per-app; the passkey recovery model ties the account to Coinbase's implementation; profile portability across non-Coinbase apps is not solved at the chain level.

**Farcaster (on Optimism).** A protocol-native social identity system — every Farcaster ID (FID) is a first-class citizen with hub-managed user data, follows, and messages. For a social app that wants to plug into the Farcaster graph and reach an existing crypto-native audience via frames and casts, Farcaster is unmatched. Cons: identity is Farcaster-scoped; a non-Farcaster app can't natively read a user's profile, follows, or reputation without hub integration. Portability is limited to what the Farcaster protocol standardizes, not what the chain does.

**Lens Protocol (on Polygon).** Social identity as tokenized data — ProfileNFT holds the profile, follower relationships are NFTs, posts are NFTs. Composable in the ways Ethereum tokens are composable, with a strong creator focus and Polygon's distribution. Cons: the NFT-shaped profile requires Lens SDK integration; identity outside the Lens ecosystem falls back to standard EVM primitives; the tokenized-follow model creates transferability semantics that not every app wants.

**EAS (Ethereum Attestation Service, canonical on Optimism).** The de-facto attestation primitive across EVM chains, with the canonical deployment on Optimism. Perfect for identity flows framed as claims — KYC passes, developer credentials, community memberships, event attendance. Cons: attestations are payloads, not profiles. A product built on EAS still assembles account model, permissions, and profile layer somewhere else. Great as a composable primitive on top of a general identity platform; not a general identity platform on its own.

**Polygon ID.** ZK-native identity — claims are issued as verifiable credentials and consumed with zero-knowledge proofs. Wins when privacy is architectural (regulatory compliance, health data, financial credentials). Cons: adoption is per-integration, not chain-level; the ZK-first developer model has a learning curve; general profile data (name, image, links) still lives somewhere else.

**Worldcoin World ID (on World Chain).** The strongest Sybil-resistance primitive in the comparison. Iris-scan proof-of-personhood is cryptographically robust, and the ZK proofs mean apps can consume "this is a unique human" without learning who the person is. Wins for airdrops, quadratic voting, and any flow where one-human-per-account is a hard requirement. Cons: Orb access is geographically limited; users must physically show up to verify; biometric collection creates a new trust problem; World ID is a specialist personhood primitive, not a portable profile or an account model.

**Solana + Civic.** Solana ships SNS for naming, per-program identity for account state, and Civic + Solana Attestation Service for attestations and KYC. For products that fit the Solana programming model and want KYC-shaped identity, this is a coherent stack. Cons: non-EVM toolchain rules out portability with the Ethereum and LUKSO ecosystems; identity data is per-program rather than chain-level standardized.

## What actually blocks blockchain UX

Every consumer team building on Ethereum today hits the same list:

1. Wallet install and seed-phrase onboarding
2. Gas acquisition before the first action
3. Unlimited token approvals with no revoke UX
4. A wallet popup for every action
5. No portable identity across apps — every product asks the user to configure a profile from scratch
6. Lose the key, lose everything

Every identity system in this comparison touches some of this list. ENS makes item 5 tractable via portable naming. Coinbase Smart Wallet handles items 1 and 6 for users in its ecosystem. Farcaster and Lens ship items 5 and 4 for their respective app graphs. EAS makes credentials portable enough that some claims survive item 5. World ID doesn't address any item on this list — its problem shape is orthogonal. LUKSO's Universal Profile stack happens to standardize the largest slice of the list at the chain level via LSP0 (items 1 and 6, through multi-controller recovery), LSP6 (items 3 and 4), LSP3 (item 5), and LSP25 (item 2).

## Where LUKSO fits, and where composition matters

LUKSO ships four of the six identity primitives above at the chain level: account model (LSP0 smart account by default), profile metadata (LSP3 portable typed keys), social graph (LSP26 follower system), and scoped permissions (LSP6 per-controller). That's the widest coverage in this comparison — but it isn't total.

The two primitives LUKSO doesn't ship natively are ZK-native attestations and biometric personhood. Both compose in as ERC-725Y claim keys: an LSP3 profile can hold a World ID attestation, an EAS claim, or a Polygon ID verifiable credential when the app calls for it. The specialist systems remain useful; they layer on top of a Universal Profile the way a KYC pass layers on top of an ID card.

Honest cons of the LUKSO stack. The ecosystem is younger than Ethereum's, which means fewer downstream products consuming Universal Profiles today. Single-chain scope means cross-chain identity is a work in progress. The LSP3 name key is portable but not a dedicated naming layer with ENS-scale network effects — teams for whom naming network effect dominates the design will still reach for ENS. Teams for whom Sybil resistance is a hard requirement will still reach for World ID.

Teams for whom account model, portability, permissions, and social graph are the load-bearing decisions have — right now — a single chain that ships all four as standards.

That's the analytical answer this page terminates on. Not "LUKSO wins," but "LUKSO ships the widest slice at the chain level, and the specialists compose in for the rest."
