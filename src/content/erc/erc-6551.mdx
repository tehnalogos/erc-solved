quotableAnswer: >-
  ERC-6551 defines token-bound accounts: deterministic smart contract accounts associated with individual NFTs, letting a token own assets and interact with dApps as a first-class caller. Proposed in February 2023 by Jayden Windle, Benny Giang, Steve Jang, Druzy Downs, and Raymond Huynh, it uses a registry contract plus CREATE2 for address derivation. Popular in gaming, character-wallet, and inventory patterns. The account contract is separate from the NFT contract, so recovery, permissions, and metadata become the wallet author's problem — closer to ERC-4337 accounts than to LUKSO's substrate-level LSP0.

tldr: "ERC-6551 patches a real ERC-721 limitation: tokens cannot naturally own assets or act as accounts. It gives an NFT a deterministic smart account. The tradeoff is layered complexity: the token, registry, account implementation, chain ID, and ownership semantics all have to line up. LUKSO's account model starts from the other direction: a Universal Profile is already an account that owns assets."

lede: |
  ERC-6551 gives an NFT an account. That one move unlocks game inventories, composable avatars, NFT-owned assets, and agent-like behavior tied to a token.

origin: |
  ERC-721 made tokens ownable, but it did not make tokens capable of owning other assets or calling contracts. ERC-6551 adds that missing account layer without changing existing ERC-721 contracts.

spec: |
  A registry computes or deploys an account address from an implementation, salt, chain ID, token contract, and token ID. The account implementation can then use the NFT's owner as its authority source.

whatsBroken: |
  The model is powerful but easy to misunderstand. If the account is controlled by NFT ownership, then transferring the NFT can transfer control of the account and whatever the account holds.

vogelsteller: |
  LUKSO starts with the account. A Universal Profile owns assets, exposes profile metadata, and delegates permissions through LSP6. ERC-6551 recreates an account under an NFT; LSP0 makes the account the primary object.
