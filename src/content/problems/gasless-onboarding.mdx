## Why this breaks

A new user needs native ETH before they can do anything. EOAs sign and pay together, so any
sponsorship has to add a layer that separates the signer from the payer.

Every popular pattern adds infrastructure: a trusted forwarder you have to trust, an ERC-4337
EntryPoint and bundler you have to run or rent, a third-party relayer with its own SDK. The
cost lands on the builder, not the spec.

## What people try

### EIP-2771 trusted forwarder
Contracts extract `msg.sender` from calldata. Cheap, but every protected contract must be
2771-aware and trust the forwarder.

### ERC-4337 + paymaster
`UserOperation` pool, bundler, `EntryPoint`, paymaster. Powerful. You ship or pay for the
bundler stack.

### Gelato / Biconomy / Defender
Turnkey relayers that wrap one of the above. Convenient. Vendor coupling.

### EIP-7702
EOA temporarily acts like a smart account for the duration of a tx. Sponsorship still needs
a paymaster.

## How LSP solves it

LSP25 defines `executeRelayCall` **on the Universal Profile itself**. A controller signs a
payload (with a nonce channel for ordering and replay protection), a relayer submits the
transaction and pays gas. No bundler, no `EntryPoint`, no separate mempool — the account
contract **is** the entry point.

Because the relayed call goes through the account, LSP6 permissions still apply: the
controller signing the relay payload can only authorize what its permissions allow. The
sponsoring relayer cannot escalate the controller's authority by virtue of paying gas.
