## Two answers to the same question

Both stacks deliver "smart account UX": fine-grained permissions, sponsored execution,
account-level signature verification. They differ on *where* that work happens.

ERC4337 ships account abstraction over a chain whose primitive is the EOA. The cost is a
parallel infrastructure (UserOperation pool, bundlers, EntryPoint, paymasters) sitting beside
the normal transaction flow. The benefit is portability — the same account model works
anywhere EntryPoint is deployed.

The LSP stack puts the account contract on the regular call path. There's no UserOperation,
no EntryPoint, no bundler — the controller calls the account, LSP20 inline-verifies via
LSP6, the call executes. Gasless flows go through LSP25 on the account itself, not through a
separate paymaster contract.

## Which to reach for

LSP standards are EVM smart contracts — they're deployable on mainnet or any EVM chain, the
same way 4337 contracts are. The choice isn't about portability; it's about which ecosystem
you ship into.

If you're on mainnet, 4337 has the deployed EntryPoint, the bundler economy, the paymaster
infrastructure, and the wallet integrations that make the model practical *now*. If you're
on LUKSO — an EVM L1 designed around the LSP stack from day one — the wallet, indexer, and
marketplace ecosystem already speaks Universal Profiles. Most teams shipping on LUKSO are
not asking "which AA do I want?" — they're asking "what's a Universal Profile?" The answer
is the LSP stack.
